Final Design

The completed ESP32 C3 Zero enclosure — all prototype issues resolved and ready for deployment.

Overview

Key features

The final design incorporates all lessons learned across both prototypes. Every problem identified in testing has been resolved, and the result is a clean, functional, and printable enclosure.

  • Precise 0.2 mm board tolerance — secure fit, no movement
  • Honeycomb vent pattern on side walls for maximum airflow
  • Recessed antenna window for optimal Wi-Fi signal
  • Reinforced snap-fit clips — reliable, easy open/close
  • Full port and button access cutouts
  • Support-free lid geometry for clean print results
  • Wall-mount slot on rear face for fixed installation

Problem resolution

How each issue was resolved

Problem identified Prototype it appeared in How it was resolved in the Final Design
Board movement inside case Prototype 1 Precision inner cavity with 0.2 mm tolerance on all sides
No ventilation / heat build-up Prototype 1 Honeycomb vent pattern — large open area, structural strength retained
USB-C port blocked Prototype 1 Chamfered opening for easy cable insertion at any angle
Buttons inaccessible Prototype 1 Recessed button guides — press-through access with no need to open case
Snap-fit clips too flexible Prototype 2 Increased clip wall thickness from 1.2 mm to 1.8 mm; added reinforcing rib
Support marks on vent slots Prototype 2 Redesigned vent geometry to 45° bridges — no supports needed
Wi-Fi range reduced by case Prototype 2 Recessed 8 mm × 12 mm antenna window cut directly above antenna position

Project conclusion

Why this design works

The final design successfully meets all original project goals.

The enclosure protects the ESP32 C3 Zero from physical damage while allowing full functionality — all ports and buttons are accessible, heat is managed effectively through the honeycomb ventilation, and the snap-fit lid provides convenient access without tools. The print geometry has been optimised to produce clean results on a standard FDM printer without any support material, reducing both print time and post-processing work.

This iterative design process — test, identify problems, revise, retest — demonstrates how physical prototyping rapidly surfaces issues that are difficult to predict in CAD alone. The three-iteration approach was essential to arriving at a reliable, polished final product.
